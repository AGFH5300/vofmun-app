Key Terms

A—Z Index

Moderated Caucus

Debate Phase Formal

A mixture of both formal and informal debate. Delegates remain in their seats and are called upon by the Chair to speak for a short period of time, enabling a more direct conversation on a specific sub-topic.

Motion to Adjourn

Procedural

A request to end the committee session entirely until the next conference. Usually reserved for the very final session of the weekend.

Yield to the Chair

Speaker's List

When a delegate finishes their speech before their time has elapsed and gives the remaining time back to the Chair, ending their turn without questions or comments.

Dais

Administration

The group of people, usually high school or college students, in charge of a MUN committee. It generally consists of a Chair, a Director, and a Rapporteur.

format_quote

"The quality of debate is directly proportional to the clarity of the language used."

Hon. Secretariat, VOFMUN 2024

Point Categories

Point of Order

Used when a delegate believes that there has been an error in the parliamentary procedure.

Point of Information

A question directed to a delegate who has just finished speaking. These are only allowed if the delegate yields to points of information.

Point of Personal Privilege

Used if a delegate experiences discomfort (e.g., room temperature, volume) that hinders their participation.

Point of Inquiry

Used to ask the Dais a question regarding the rules of procedure or a point of clarification.

Delegation: United Kingdom
Committee: Security Council
Duration: ~4 Minutes

Honorable Chair, Distinguished Delegates,

The United Kingdom stands before this assembly today to address a matter of urgent concern regarding international maritime law and the sovereignty of nations. We must look beyond the immediate tactical gains of territorial posturing and focus on the enduring stability provided by the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS).

It is our firm belief that diplomacy is not merely the absence of conflict, but the active pursuit of consensus through established legal frameworks. The recent developments in the South China Sea represent a departure from these norms that we cannot overlook.

"Let us remember that international law is not a menu from which nations can pick and choose based on convenience."

We propose a three-pillar framework for immediate de-escalation:

  • Re-affirmation of the 2016 Arbitral Tribunal rulings.
  • Establishment of a joint-monitored corridor for non-military merchant vessels.
  • A moratorium on artificial island construction within disputed EEZs.

I yield the remainder of my time to the chair.
